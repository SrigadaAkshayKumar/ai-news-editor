Here's what happened in tech in the last 24 hours. Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark told the world's finance officials that AI could make the US economy almost 50% bigger by 2030. Meta took the word "train" out of its new rules for crawling websites after publishers pushed back, and Nvidia says its top gaming card, the RTX 5090, is not discontinued, even as it disappears from store shelves. Let's get into it. Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark spoke on Saturday at the annual meetings of the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank in Bangkok, according to Reuters. Clark said one internal Anthropic model shows that, under certain conditions, US economic output could rise by almost 50% by 2030. That means about $44 trillion, up from just over $30 trillion today. Clark said it sounds very outlandish. He asked governments to agree on what he called unusual and serious rules for AI, and said the window to put safeguards in place is now a small number of years. Why it matters? Reuters reports Anthropic is preparing to go public, and its leaders are pitching both the upside and the risks to the people who run the world economy. Meta has changed the wording of its new commercial terms after a backlash from publishers. The terms cover how Meta's software crawls websites linked to businesses that use its products. The earlier draft said, "Meta could use that content to provide, develop, improve research, and train its products." The word "train" is now gone. Meta says the crawling terms only ever applied to its ads and business products, and that it has now spelled that out. The terms take effect on the 30th of October. Publishers are not fully satisfied. The News Media Alliance says training was removed from the list, but not expressly banned. For anyone who runs a website, it shows how closely the fine print on AI training is now being read. Nvidia says its flagship graphics card, the RTX 5090, has not been discontinued. Jeff Fisher, a senior vice president at Nvidia, told Taiwanese media in Taipei on Friday that the company will keep shipping it. The denial follows reports from retailers and partners that new chips for the card had dried up. The card has dropped off Nvidia's own online store in the US, and Micro Center lists several models as not carried. Tom's Hardware and Hardware Busters report third-party sellers on Amazon asking around $8,500 against an official price of $1,999. Fisher admitted that supply is constrained. For gamers, the takeaway is simple. Not discontinued is not the same as available. Anthropic has published a report on how AI is changing jobs inside its own company, and TechCrunch covered it on Saturday. Since early 2025, the share of junior staff in Anthropic's technical teams has fallen from 21% to 14%. Among new technical hires, the junior share fell from 27% to 9%. Anthropic says code commits per engineer are now five times what they were six months earlier, and the median employee spends 20% more time in meetings than a year ago. Anthropic says human attention and judgment are becoming the bottleneck. It is one company's data, but for students and early career developers, it is a clear signal of where entry-level hiring could be heading. In India, defence Technology Company, Zen Technologies is buying a majority stake in an AI company. Its board approved the deal on Saturday, according to a filing with the National Stock Exchange. Zen will take 51.98% of Bengaluru-based Cogknit semantics for up to about 135 crore rupees in cash. Cogknit builds AI-based battlefield management and situational awareness systems for the armed forces. The deal is expected to close by the 13th of December. Cogknit is small. Its turnover last financial year was under half a crore rupees. Why it matters, Zen is adding AI software to a business built on training simulators and anti-drone systems and as a lighter one to finish. Mercedes-AMG unveiled its new electric GT SUV on Saturday. The top version, the GT63, makes up to 1,153 horsepower from three axial flux electric motors. Its battery can charge at up to 710 kilowatts and Mercedes says 10-80% takes about 12 minutes. Electrek reports the first cars should reach US dealers in summer 2027. The price has not been announced. Beyond the horsepower, the real story is charging. Cars that can take this much power will push charging networks to keep up. That was the day.
